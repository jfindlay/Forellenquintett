# Arranging notes: Violin II from the double bass part

Schubert's D. 667 is scored Vn, Va, Vc, Cb, Pf. This arrangement is for piano + string quartet (Vn1,
Vn2, Va, Vc). Vn1, Va, Vc and Pf read the published urtext unchanged; the only product of this
project is a **Violin II part derived from the *Basso***, and thus has to work with the unmodified
other four parts.

If a traditional string quartet + piano arrangement is desired that attempts to preserve the
timbral, voice, and thematic intent of Schubert's original composition, the user should try the
[Jansen](https://imslp.org/wiki/Special:ReverseLookup/722150) arrangement instead.  Jansen keeps
only the Pf and Vn1 parts unmodified and the lower three string parts are all adjusted to cover the
missing Cb.

This arrangement is a deliberate departure from the bass sound of the original, so rather than try
to simulate the missing bass voice by shuffling the other voices, the part is given directly to Vn2.
The rebalanced ensemble timbre and voicing does not apologize for the change but invites the players
and listeners to rediscover this timeless work which has much to offer even without Schubert's
original instrumentation.  In some ways, an experienced listener may even appreciate that this
version gives a unique perspective on the bass part by outlining it with an ebullient violin sound
where appropriate.  Not a single note or rest was modified from the urtext, upto octave equivalence.
Both violin and bass belonging to the same instrument family, articulation was also preserved and is
the same as can be rendered between the two instruments.

The most dramatic modification is in mvmt IV at the first part of variation IV.  The Vn2 part is
lifted an extra octave higher than it could have been and it rises well above the Vn1 line here.  It
is true the part is doubling the cello and Schubert wanted the moving line in the low voices but
both are marked *ff* and the theme can easily be lost if the Vc and Cb (or Vn2 and Vc) players
aren't aggressively bringing it out over the sextuplet hammering in the other voices, also marked
*ff*.  The violin's natural flexibility and gracefulness adds a magnificent edge here, which could
be construed the midpoint climax for the whole piece.

The bass sounds an octave below written pitch, the cello plays at written pitch, and the violin's
lowest note is G3.  Lifted one or two octaves from the Basso's written pitch, the violin II part
lands one or two octaves above the cello wherever the two share a line, and two or three octaves
above the bass's sounding pitch. At best it reproduces the bass line's *rhythm, articulation,
pizzicati and thematic exchanges* as an inner voice, in the viola's register.  What the bass
contributed as 16-foot weight is not replaced; the cello and the piano left hand are left without
it.  This arrangement can thus be considered an inversion or permutation of the parts in a way that
Bach might experiment.  Schubert's genius is still clearly heard and hopefully the novelty brings
joy to listeners and the utility brings possibility to players when a bass cannot be found.
